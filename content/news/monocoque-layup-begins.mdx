Layup started on Tuesday morning. After eleven weeks of laminate iteration and a design
review that ran three hours over its slot, the 2027 tub is finally on the mould.

## What changed

Last season's monocoque passed structural equivalency comfortably, but it did so by
being heavier than it needed to be — we carried a uniform laminate through regions that
the FEA had been telling us for months were nowhere near limit load. This year the
schedule is zoned. The side impact structure and the front bulkhead keep their full
stack; the upper surfaces and the seatback drop two plies each.

The measured saving on the coupon programme is 3.4 kg against last year's tub, with no
reduction in the equivalency margins we submit to the IMechE. That is roughly the mass
of the entire rear wing assembly, recovered from a laminate spreadsheet.

## Why the tooling took so long

We moved from a machined MDF pattern to a CNC-cut tooling board this season, which cost
us four weeks of lead time and a difficult conversation about budget. It buys surface
finish that survives more than one cure cycle, which means the B-surface no longer needs
a filler pass before bonding. Whether that trade was worth it is a question for the
post-season review, but the first pull off the mould looks considerably better than
anything we have produced before.

## Next

Autoclave cure is booked with our composites partner for the first week of September. Bonded
inserts and suspension pickups follow through the month, and the tub should be ready for
the first suspension trial fit by early October.
